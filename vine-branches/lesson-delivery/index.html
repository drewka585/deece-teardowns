How the daily lesson arrives

A full lesson runs about 5,000 characters. The messaging platform will not carry that in one piece, so the lesson has to arrive as a sequence. Here is how each version actually reads at 6am on a member's phone, and what each one costs you to run.

The rule
behind this

The platform draws a hard line between a message you start and a message you send back. When a member writes to you, a 24 hour reply window opens, and inside it you can send anything — written fresh for that person, up to 4,096 characters a message, at no per-message cost.

Outside that window, every message you start has to use a body the provider approved in advance, capped at 1,024 characters, billed per send. Approved bodies still take variables, so the member's name, their verse and yesterday's journal line all drop in per person. What they cannot do is let the system write the whole thing freely.

The 6am lesson is a message we start, a day after the member last wrote. So the only real question is how much of the lesson rides in the approved opener, and how much waits for a reply. That is what the three options below trade against each other.

Against the alternative

The proposal promised you a look at plain SMS as well, since it has no approval step at all.

OptionAt 6amNeeds a replyApproval stepPer member / moReal cost to you
A · All approved 6 messagesNo~14 bodies$1.80 – 2.70 Lessons get locked into a fixed skeleton. Every wording change waits on review.
B · Knock first 1 messageYes, always~3 bodies$0.30 – 0.45 A member who does not reply gets nothing that day. On a devotional, that is most people some mornings.
C · Standalone opener 1 messageOnly for the rest~4 bodies$0.30 – 0.45 The opener's shape is fixed. The verse, the teaching and the journal callback still change daily.
SMS via Twilio 1 long textNoNone$7.00 – 8.00 No approvals, but roughly 33 billed segments a lesson, no audio, no read state, and no journal thread.

SMS also loses the thing the whole platform is built on: a single running conversation the member can scroll back through, and that the journal, matching and crisis checks all read from.

What I would build

Option C. It is the only one where a member who never replies still gets scripture and a real thought at 6am, and a member who does reply gets a conversation rather than a broadcast.

It also does something the other two do not. The reply is the engagement signal the rest of the platform needs — Workflow 7's weekly progression, the mentor matching, and the Tier 2 introduction timed off real engagement rather than a day count all need to know who is actually showing up. Option A gets you delivery with no signal. Option C gets you both from the same message.

Option B is the cheapest and the most flexible, and if the opener were anything other than a morning devotional I would probably pick it. But a knock that carries nothing is easy to leave unopened, and the day it goes unopened is the day the member got nothing at all.
