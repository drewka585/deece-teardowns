How the daily lesson arrives

A full lesson runs about 5,000 characters. Meta will not carry that in one piece, so the lesson has to arrive as a sequence. Here is how each version actually reads at 6am on a member's phone, and what each one costs you to run.

The rule
behind this

Meta runs this channel, and they treat two kinds of message completely differently. Almost everything on this page falls out of that one split.

Before they reply

A message you start

Wording
Must match a shape Meta approved
Length
1,024 characters
Cost
A cent or two each

After they reply

The 24 hour window

Wording
Anything you want
Length
4,096 characters
Cost
Free

The window opens the moment a member writes to you, and stays open 24 hours.

Approved once, not every time. You send Meta the shape of a message, with blanks in it, like Good morning, {{1}}. Day {{2}} of your walk through {{3}}. Meta checks that shape a single time, usually within minutes. Then it is yours for good and the system fills the blanks differently every morning. Nobody reads a member's lesson before it sends. Not Meta, not your staff, not us.

The blanks can say anything. The verse, the teaching, a line answering what the member wrote last night, all of it written fresh that morning. Only the words around the blanks stay put. The one limit is that a shape cannot be all blanks, because then there would be nothing for Meta to check.

The 6am lesson is a message we start, a day after the member last wrote. So the only real question here is how much of the lesson rides in the approved opener, and how much waits for a reply.

The cost ranges are wide for one reason. Meta prices a message you start by the category it assigns your template, and a daily devotional could land in either of two bands roughly six times apart. The low end of each range assumes the cheaper band, the high end assumes the dearer one. That call is Meta's, not ours, so it gets confirmed with a live test template in Milestone 1 before anything is built on top of it. Replies inside the 24 hour window are free in every case, which is why the options that send one message a day sit so far below the one that sends six.

All four, side by side

A, B and C all run on Meta and differ only in how much of the lesson rides in the approved opener. D leaves Meta entirely for plain SMS through Twilio. The proposal promised you a look at it, so here it is on the same terms, including the part that is easy to miss: SMS is not the approval-free option.

US carriers require brand and campaign registration before a single text sends, and it is still roughly 33 billed segments a lesson, with no audio, no read state and no journal thread.

D is the honest comparison, not a straw man, and it is the one option that gets worse the closer you look. It does not remove the approval step, it swaps a one time review by Meta for US carrier registration, which vets the brand and the campaign before a single message sends and blocks unregistered traffic. On top of that it runs more than ten times the cost of C, and it loses the thing the whole platform is built on, which is one continuous conversation the member can scroll back through, and that the journal, the matching and the crisis checks all read from.

What I would build

Option C. It is the only one where a member who never replies still gets scripture and a real thought at 6am, and a member who does reply gets a conversation rather than a broadcast.

It also does something the other two do not. The reply is the engagement signal the rest of the platform needs — Workflow 7's weekly progression, the mentor matching, and the Tier 2 introduction timed off real engagement rather than a day count all need to know who is actually showing up. Option A gets you delivery with no signal. Option C gets you both from the same message.

Option B is the cheapest and the most flexible, and if the opener were anything other than a morning devotional I would probably pick it. But a knock that carries nothing is easy to leave unopened, and the day it goes unopened is the day the member got nothing at all.
