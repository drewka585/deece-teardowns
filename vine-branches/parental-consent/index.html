Parental consent

A member aged 13 to 17 cannot be switched on until a guardian has said yes. The awkward part is that WhatsApp will not let us message a guardian who has never messaged us first, so consent cannot simply be a message we send. Here are the two routes, side by side, and a third that I think is the right answer.

Three things have to be true at the end

Every option below is really an argument about which of these you get. None is optional, which makes the choice narrower than it first looks.

A guardian actually saw it
Not "a number replied yes". A person read what their child will receive daily, who can see the journal, and what happens if the crisis check fires. Consent to something nobody read is not consent.
There is a record
Something you could produce a year later if a guardian says they never agreed. A timestamp, the exact wording they saw, and what they typed.
The guardian's number works
This is the one that gets forgotten. Workflow 13 sends a crisis alert to the guardian of a minor. A number nobody ever verified is a number that fails on the single night the whole safeguarding design exists for.

Option A · The guardian texts in

The teenager signs up and is held pending. They are given a short code and told a parent or guardian has to send it from their own phone. The guardian opens WhatsApp and sends it, and because they messaged us first, everything after that is an ordinary conversation with no template and no cost.

Number verified
By construction. They messaged from it, so the crisis alert has somewhere real to land. This is the option's whole strength, and it is not a small one.
Disclosure is thin
Four chat bubbles is not a consent document. It is what fits before a person stops reading, and there is no version of it that includes a data policy.
The record is weak
You have "a number sent I CONSENT on this date". You cannot show what wording was on screen at the time, if the copy has changed since.
Trivially faked
A fifteen year old with a second number, or a willing friend, clears this in a minute. Nothing in the flow tests that the sender is a parent.
Cost to build

Lowest of the three by some distance. No web page, no email, no template. It is a keyword handler on the number you already have, and it is the only option that adds nothing to the monthly bill.

Option B · The guardian signs on a page

The teenager enters a guardian's name and email. We send that address a one-time link. The guardian opens a page that explains the whole thing properly, types their name, and confirms. The teenager is activated when the page is submitted, not before.

Consent for Marcus Reed, age 15

Grace Family Church would like Marcus to join Vine and Branches, a daily discipleship programme delivered over WhatsApp.

What he receives. One short Bible teaching each weekday morning, and an invitation each evening to write a private reflection.

Who can read it. Named pastoral staff at Grace Family Church. Not other members, and not other churches.

If he is at risk. Every reflection is checked. If anything suggests Marcus may be in danger, church staff are alerted immediately, and so are you.

Stopping. You can end this at any time by replying STOP from your phone, or by contacting the church.

The disclosure is real
There is room to say all of it, including the crisis policy, in language a parent can act on. None of this fits in a chat bubble.
The record is defensible
Timestamp, the exact wording served, the typed name, and which link was used and when it was issued. This is a document rather than a reply.
The number is unverified
Whatever phone number ends up on the record was typed in by the teenager. The crisis alert depends on it, and nothing in this flow has ever sent anything to it successfully.
Email gets missed
Consent now depends on a parent finding an email from a sender they do not recognise. Expect to resend, and expect some never to land at all.
Cost to build

A hosted page, a signed single-use link, and an email sender. Modest, and it is work that has to happen anyway if you ever want a paper trail.

Option C · The page for the record, WhatsApp to bind the number

Option A verifies a phone number but cannot properly disclose. Option B discloses properly but leaves the phone number unverified. They fail in opposite directions, which is the clue: run both, in that order, and each covers the other's hole.

1

The page

The guardian opens the emailed link, reads the disclosure, types their name. This produces the record. The teenager is not activated yet.

2

Their phone

The page finishes by showing a code and a WhatsApp button. The guardian sends it from the handset they want to be reached on.

3

Activated

The record and the number now belong to the same act of consent, and the crisis alert has reached a real handset once before it was ever needed.

The crisis alert is what decides this. Everything else in the comparison is a matter of degree, and either of the first two options would be defensible on its own terms. But Workflow 13 messages the guardian of a minor at the worst moment of that family's year. It does it automatically, at 2am, with nobody checking the number first. A consent flow that never sent anything to that number is a flow that finds out it was wrong on the only night it mattered.

Option C is one extra step for the guardian, taken while they are already sitting in front of the page, and it turns an untested number into a tested one.

Worth saying plainly

None of the three proves the person consenting is the parent. A determined teenager defeats all of them, and that is true of every consent flow of this kind, including the ones large platforms use. What C buys you is not certainty. It is that the consent was informed, that there is a record of it, and that the number you will rely on in an emergency has been used successfully at least once.

What I need from you before this gets built

The consent wording
The page text above is mine, written so you have something concrete to react to rather than a description of something. The words a guardian actually agrees to should be yours, and if your denomination or your insurer has a form for this, that wins over anything I would write.
The age range
Built for 13 to 17, on the assumption that under 13 is simply not permitted. Confirm that, because if under 13 is allowed with consent there is a third state to handle rather than a rejection.
Guardian as payer
If a minor's subscription has to be paid by the guardian, the cleanest moment to collect it is the end of the consent page, while they are already there and already agreeing. Say the word and it goes into the same flow.
