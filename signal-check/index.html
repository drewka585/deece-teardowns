How this actually works

Open the method and the raw numbers

Nothing here is a canned number. When you loaded this page, your browser opened real connections to our server and we timed them. Every figure above comes out of those timings. Change networks and the results change, which is the easiest way to check us: run it on your wifi, then turn wifi off and run it on cellular. The numbers will move.

  1. We send seven tiny requests to our server and time each round trip. The first is thrown away, because it pays for connection setup rather than measuring your network. We take the median of the rest, so one hiccup cannot skew it.
  2. We download an 800 KB file and time it. That is roughly what a loaded Google review page weighs. The file is random data, so it cannot be compressed and the bytes on the wire are the bytes we counted.
  3. Time to a usable review page is those two measurements added up. Four round trips for connection setup and the page itself, plus the measured transfer time, plus a third of a second for the phone to draw it.

What we cannot do is load Google's own page inside this one and time that directly. Browsers block that on purpose. So we measure your connection carrying the same weight of traffic, from the device in your hand, standing where you are standing.
