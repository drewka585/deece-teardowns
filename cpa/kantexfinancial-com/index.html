3 gaps I'd close first · tap to expand
New clients ride on referrals you cannot turn up on demand▼
What's there now
Referrals and word of mouth bring in good, well-fit clients. That is a sign the work is strong.
What it's costing
Growth is capped by whoever happens to refer you this quarter. Your advisory and CFO services can serve far more owners than the referral stream delivers, so the calendar stays lumpy and hard to plan around.
Quick fix
Add one repeatable outbound channel that points ideal owners, property managers, real estate operators, and founders in your niches, straight at the advisory pages. It runs in the background so growth stops depending on this month's referrals.
The service pages inform, but do not convert▼
What's there now
Clear, well-organized descriptions of Profit First, outsourced CFO, QuickBooks cleanup, and AP work.
What it's costing
An owner who is ready to talk has to hunt for the next step. Every service page without a visible, low-friction way to book a call quietly leaks people who were one click from a conversation.
Quick fix
One consistent booking step and a short intake on each service page, so interest turns into a booked call the moment it peaks instead of an hour later when the tab is closed.
Invisible where owners first look▼
What's there now
Strong national positioning and a certification most local firms do not carry.
What it's costing
When a Topeka or regional owner searches for an accountant, the local pack is owned by firms with review moats. Sparks sits at 32 reviews, Accounting Center at 10. Kantex is not in that list, so a free, compounding discovery channel goes unused.
Quick fix
Claim and fill the Google Business Profile, then run a simple review ask to your happiest current clients. Low effort, and it compounds every month once it is running.
